Delegating Authority to AI Agents

Give an agent a narrow, time-boxed, revocable slice of your authority — provable to anyone, enforced at runtime, fully audited.

The missing trust layer for agentic AI is not “can this agent reach the tool?” — it is “is this agent authorized to do this, on whose behalf, and within what limits?” We answer that with a government-trust EU Digital Identity mandate the agent carries, and a Mandamus/WAUTH runtime that enforces it and receipts every action.

Two worlds, bridged — the moat

EU Digital Identity — portable proof

The agent carries a verifiable power-of-representation mandate credential. Any EU relying party can verify its delegated authority with standard wallet protocols. Authority becomes interoperable.

Mandamus / WAUTH — enforced authority

Governs the agent as it acts: the authorization gate, human step-up (iProov) for consequential actions, and tamper-evident signed receipts. Authority becomes accountable.

The EU credential is the verifiable face of a Mandamus mandate. Competitors do one side; together they do both.

Guarantees that matter

  • An agent can never exceed its grantMonotonic narrowing — a delegate holds only a subset of the delegator’s powers. Proven in Lean.
  • Post-quantum from day oneHybrid-PQ on the most sensitive new authority surface — ahead of the EU itself.
  • Human-in-the-loop where it countsHigh-consequence actions require a fresh iProov biometric, bound into the authorization.
  • Instant kill-switchOne tap revokes an agent; it stops being verifiable immediately (status-list revocation).
  • Every action is receiptedA hash-chained audit trail — accountability record and reputation signal in one.

The agent — just runs

A Mac app, an iPhone app, or in the cloud. Its key is hardware-protected — Secure Enclave on device; HSM/TEE with remote attestation in the cloud. The mandate is bound to that key, so it can’t be lent out.

Honest positioning

The EU has the legal hook for mandates (Reg. 2024/1183, Annex VI) but no standard yet for delegating to a machine agent. That is the gap we fill — as a formally-verified, post-quantum extension, ready to converge when the EU rulebook lands.
